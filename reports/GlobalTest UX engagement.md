# Show the work, then make results last

The best UX for GlobalTest keeps today's input-first hero and changes two things: what people see during the 30–60 second wait, and what survives after it. The wait is the bigger problem. The current card, a progress bar with "Xs · usually under a minute", matches the "blind" condition in Buell & Norton's labor-illusion experiments. In those experiments a plain progress bar lost to an instant competitor in **77% of choices at 60 s**. A live list of the actual work being done held **62–63% preference at both 30 and 60 s**. GlobalTest's steps are real (a container starts, Chrome loads the page, a screenshot is captured), so it can show them honestly. Results are the second problem. With 1-day deletion, no share links and no history, GlobalTest has only the first step of the loop that made free tools such as HubSpot Website Grader and Mozilla Observatory grow: a free, instant result. It lacks the rest of that loop, which is a shareable or re-testable artifact plus an optional email or monitor that brings people back. The highest-value changes are a per-country stage list, a title and favicon that show progress while the visitor is in another tab, a one-line summary of results, a re-test button, and share links that outlive the heavy files. Little of the evidence measures retention directly. The waiting-state advice rests on controlled studies. The retention advice rests on competitor patterns and self-reported case studies, and this report labels which is which.

## Competitors use one field, collapsed defaults and paid repeat use

Comparable tools agree on the input step. GTmetrix shows one URL field, a "Test Now" button and a collapsed summary of its defaults ("Seattle, WA, USA · Chrome · Unthrottled Connection · Change Options"). That way visitors can see what will happen without facing open controls ([GTmetrix](https://gtmetrix.com/)). Geo-screenshot tools differ mainly in how they show location choice. LocaBrowser uses a "0 / 6" counter with a hard cap ([LocaBrowser](https://www.locabrowser.com/)), BrowserStack a "15/25" counter ([BrowserStack Screenshots](https://www.browserstack.com/screenshots)), and GeoPeeker's homepage shows only a URL field and an arrow button over a decorative dotted map ([GeoPeeker](https://geopeeker.com/)). GlobalTest's "Test 3 countries" button already includes the selection count in its label, which is the same idea as those counters. Its clickable-pin map does more than GTmetrix's animated pulse map or GeoPeeker's dots, which are decoration rather than pickers.

Waiting states are the least documented part of these tools. Two patterns are confirmed. WebPageTest shows a text queue position ("Waiting behind 6 other tests…"), and its forum users got confused when that number went up without explanation ([WPT forum](https://forums.webpagetest.org/t/waiting-in-test-queue-taking-a-long-time/8210)). BrowserStack Screenshots is closest to GlobalTest's case. It shows a per-job counter ("0 of 12 done. 0 timed out. 0 stopped."), thumbnails that fill in as each one finishes, a Stop control and an option to email the results ([BrowserStack Screenshots](https://www.browserstack.com/screenshots)).

Results pages in performance tools lead with **one headline judgement**: GTmetrix's A–F grade, PageSpeed Insights' Core Web Vitals pass/fail, or WebPageTest's "Is it Quick / Usable / Resilient?" cards. Metrics, filmstrips and detail tabs follow ([GTmetrix blog](https://gtmetrix.com/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-new-gtmetrix-report-powered-by-lighthouse/); [Catchpoint WPT docs](https://docs.catchpoint.com/wpt/docs/interpreting-instant-test-results.md)). Geo-screenshot tools stop at a static side-by-side grid. In the tools surveyed, none pairs per-country screenshots with load times and a scroll video, and none summarizes differences between countries. That is a possible differentiator for GlobalTest, though competitor coverage in this research was limited.

All of these tools give away a one-off test and charge for **repeat use over time**: monitoring and alerts, longer history, more locations and queue priority ([GTmetrix Pricing](https://gtmetrix.com/pricing/)). GeoPeeker is the closest analogue. Its Quick Peeks need no account and cover six locations. A free account adds one uptime monitor and **30-day retention**, and a Solo trial needs no card ([GeoPeeker Pricing](https://geopeeker.com/pricing)). GTmetrix shows what goes wrong when a tool gates too early. Trustpilot reviewers in 2023–24 complained that you "MUST get an account" to see results and were then capped at five tests ([Trustpilot](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/gtmetrix.com)).

## A blind progress bar loses at 60 s, and showing the real work wins

A 30–60 second wait is far beyond the **10-second limit** for holding attention. After 10 s, users switch to other tasks and have to reorient when they come back, and NN/g recommends a percent-done indicator plus a way to stop for anything longer ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)). Feedback helps a lot. Users shown a moving progress bar waited on average **3x longer** than users shown nothing ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators/)). The same bar is weak against the alternative, though. In Buell & Norton's travel-search experiments, people chose between an instant site and a waiting site that returned identical results. With a uniform progress bar, **42% chose the waiting site at 30 s and 23% at 60 s**. With a live tally of the work being done, **62% chose it at 30 s and 63% at 60 s**. Telling people the list of tasks up front, without showing the work live, did not produce the benefit ([Buell & Norton 2011](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/Norton_Michael_The%20labor%20illusion%20How%20operational_f4269b70-3732-4fc4-8113-72d0c47533e0.pdf)). Maister's older propositions point the same way: unexplained and uncertain waits feel longer than explained, finite ones ([Maister](https://davidmaister.com/blog/201/The-Psychology-of-Waiting-Lines.html)).

The shape of the progress bar matters too, and the studies conflict in a way that turns out to be useful. A meta-analysis of 32 web-survey experiments found that a constant-speed indicator did **not** reduce drop-off. Bars that moved fast early and slower later reduced drop-off, and bars that started slow increased it ([Villar et al. 2013](https://research.google/pubs/where-am-i-a-meta-analysis-of-experiments-on-the-effects-of-progress-indicators-for-web-surveys/)). In Harrison's lab studies, pauses made a bar feel longer and acceleration was strongly preferred. Both effects were strongest **near the end** of the process ([Harrison et al. 2007](https://chrisharrison.net/projects/progressbars/ProgBarHarrison.pdf)). The two results fit together: show visible progress early so people don't quit, and never stall near the end, because the ending shapes how the wait is remembered. Harrison's bars lasted only 5–15 s, so applying them to 60 s is an assumption.

There are three caveats. First, transparency has diminishing returns: its benefit started to decline after about 30 s in the travel study. Second, it backfires when the outcome disappoints. In Buell & Norton's dating experiment, a transparent 30 s wait followed by a bad result was rated *lower* than instant service (2.10 vs 3.24) ([Buell & Norton 2011](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/Norton_Michael_The%20labor%20illusion%20How%20operational_f4269b70-3732-4fc4-8113-72d0c47533e0.pdf)). For GlobalTest, a timeout or blocked page after a long visible wait needs a clear explanation and a quick retry. Third, skeleton screens don't help here. NN/g limits them to full-page loads under 10 s ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/)), and in Viget's 136-person test the skeleton screen scored worst on perceived speed ([Viget](https://viget.com/articles/a-bone-to-pick-with-skeleton-screens)). The progress signals should also be truthful. Smoothing the bar between real checkpoints is fine, but invented stages are not. Adar's ground rules allow benevolent deception only when users would prefer it and it measurably helps ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=164609)), and a testing tool depends on being trusted for accuracy.

Products with long waits have settled on similar patterns: live, sectioned step logs in Netlify and Vercel ([Netlify changelog](https://www.netlify.com/changelog/2026-04-06-deploy-logs-streaming-is-now-faster/); [Vercel Academy](https://vercel.com/academy/vercel-foundations/logs)), progressive previews in AI image generation ([OpenAI API reference](https://developers.openai.com/api/reference/resources/images/generation-streaming-events)), and queue position at WebPageTest. These are patterns only. None of these companies has published outcome data. Several other ideas are reasonable but untested: showing status in the tab title and favicon, a pulsing map pin, tips during the wait, and browsing examples while waiting. The research found no study measuring any of them. The case for title and favicon status rests on NN/g's point that most users switch tasks after 10 s. If GlobalTest offers browser notifications, they should come from an explicit button, because browsers throttle permission prompts and a denial is effectively permanent ([Braze docs](https://www.braze.com/docs/developer_guide/push_notifications/soft_push_prompts/?sdktab=web)).

## Results that last 1 day lose both sharing and return visits

The free-tool case studies with numbers all follow one path: a free, instant, no-login result, then an artifact people can share or rank, then backlinks, then an optional email or account for persistence. HubSpot Website Grader graded about **4 million sites** between 2006 and 2011 ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/hubspot-launches-comprehensive-free-marketing-grader-tool-after-grading-4-million-websites-135091323.html)). A teardown credits it with **40,000+ backlinks** from users posting their scores, but that figure is the teardown's own claim with no primary source ([Startup Spells](https://startupspells.com/p/hubspot-website-grader-seo-win-free-tools-marketing)). Mozilla Observatory reported that **over 2,500 of 50,000+ sites** moved from a failing grade to A/A+ through repeated scan, fix and rescan cycles ([Mozilla Security Blog](https://blog.mozilla.org/security/2017/06/28/analysis-alexa-top-1m-sites/)). That is observational evidence, but it is the clearest sign that an easy re-test drives repeat use. Wordle's growth after it added a share button, from about 90 players to over 2 million in roughly two months, shows how much a visual, spoiler-free shareable result can matter, though it comes from a very different category ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/39248)).

GlobalTest's output is easy to share in principle: "my site in five countries" is visual, much like Wordle's grid. But the product has no link to share. GTmetrix reports live at permanent URLs, and a `/latest` suffix always points to the newest report for a site ([GTmetrix blog](https://gtmetrix.com/blog/?p=8230)). SSL Labs results are publicly cached by default ([gf.dev](https://gf.dev/learn/ssl-labs-a-plus)). Evidence that preview images increase clicks is old and directional. EdgeRank Checker found that larger images on Facebook link posts got about **69% more clicks** ([Chief Marketer](https://chiefmarketer.com/new-bigger-link-images-on-facebook-yield-69-increase-in-clicks-for-shared-links)). The 30–40% figures that circulate for OG cards are unverified vendor claims. The 1-day deletion policy conflicts with permalinks: a shared link that returns a 404 the next day breaks the loop. GlobalTest can keep the lightweight summary page and thumbnails longer while still deleting the heavy videos quickly. Alternatively, an expired link can open a page that pre-fills the same URL and countries, so a dead link becomes a new test. Results should be unlisted by default rather than public, because people test staging and unreleased URLs.

The return mechanism with the clearest logic is **scheduled monitoring with email alerts**, because it brings people back without them having to remember. GTmetrix's free account includes daily, weekly or monthly monitoring, and its alert emails link straight to a report ([GTmetrix](https://gtmetrix.com/)). UptimeRobot's free monitoring tier is described as its main growth driver, though that description comes from a competitor ([HostTracker](https://www.host-tracker.com/blog/uptimerobot-alternative)). The guidance on gating is consistent. NN/g has opposed upfront login walls since 1999 and says they are especially costly on sites people visit rarely ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/login-walls/)). So email should unlock persistence after the visitor has seen value, and should never be required to see the first screenshots. GlobalTest has natural alert triggers: a country starts returning an error or block page, a new redirect appears, load time gets worse, or the screenshot changes visibly.

On the results page itself, a one-line verdict above the grid would match how performance tools lead with a single judgement. An example: "Loaded in 3 of 3 countries · fastest Germany 1.2 s · slowest India 4.2 s · redirected in 1". GlobalTest can compute these values cheaply: the final URL after redirects, `<html lang>`, the HTTP status and the load time. No geo-screenshot tool surveyed shows such a summary. A letter grade would also be shareable, and Mozilla's numbers suggest grades motivate re-testing. But a grade that penalizes intentional localization, such as a different language per country, would mislead users, so those differences should appear as facts and not as deductions. Comparison views (side-by-side with synced zoom, a drag slider, or an onion-skin overlay) are standard in visual-diff tools ([CrossBrowserTesting](https://help.crossbrowsertesting.com/screenshots/general/screenshot-regression-testing/)). No source measures their effect on engagement.

## The hero passes the 10-second test, but its proof sits below the fold

The chance that a visitor leaves is highest in the first 10 seconds, and pages that hold someone for 30 seconds often keep them for 2 minutes or more ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/)). That makes the wait itself a retention risk: a visitor who has started a test is engaged, and losing them before the first result costs more than losing a casual browser. Above-the-fold content still gets **about 57% of viewing time**, and attention drops sharply below it ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)). Unbounce's dataset of 57 million conversions found that pages written at a **5th–7th grade reading level converted nearly twice as well** as pages written at a professional level ([DMN summary](https://dmn.ca/unbounces-2024-conversion-benchmark-report-proves-that-attention-spans-are-declining-and-so-are-conversion-rates/)). GlobalTest's headline, "See how your site looks and loads in any country", is plain and concrete, and the input-first layout fits the evidence. The weak spot is proof. The CNN, Google and Facebook examples sit below the map, where they get little attention. Vendor data from interactive-demo companies claims about 3x conversion for demos over static pages ([Navattic](https://navattic.com/report/state-of-the-interactive-product-demo-2025)), but those figures come from B2B sales funnels and are self-reported.

The research supports a few friction fixes. NN/g's guidance on progressive disclosure is to show the important options first and defer the rest behind a labelled control ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)). Good defaults should be visible and clearly editable ([Tidwell](https://www.mit.edu/%7Ejtidwell/language/good_defaults.html)). The "choice overload" effect is less reliable than its reputation: a 50-experiment meta-analysis found a mean effect of almost zero ([Scheibehenne et al. 2010](https://abcwest.cogs.indiana.edu/pmwiki/pdf/scheibehenne.options.2010.pdf)). So the issue with the chips and option cards is how much space they take in the first view, not how many there are. For the URL field, Baymard recommends validating on blur rather than while the user types ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/inline-form-validation)). NN/g found that placeholders used as the only label hurt usability ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/form-design-placeholders/)). `type="url"` rejects bare domains such as "cnn.com" unless the code handles them, and iOS zooms into inputs with text under 16px ([Website Spec](https://specification.website/spec/accessibility/mobile-form-inputs/)). Mobile brings nearly 5x more traffic than desktop in Unbounce's data but converts worse ([Hartzer summary](https://www.billhartzer.com/pay-per-click/shocking-decline-in-attention-spans-unbounces-2024-report-reveals-critical-mistakes-that-are-hurting-your-conversion-rates/)).

Evidence for social proof is mixed. GoodUI records a leaked Airbnb test in which numeric social-proof statements were rejected ([GoodUI](https://dev.goodui.org/patterns/7)). In a roundup of more than 100 hero-image tests, 70% made no measurable difference ([Mida](https://mida.so/change-types/hero-image)). A "tests run this week" counter is therefore something to A/B test once the numbers are impressive, not a default. In our opinion, the trust signal that matters more for a URL-submitting tool is short microcopy next to the field that answers what visitors worry about: free, no sign-up, public pages only, deleted after a day. That copy must match what the backend actually does. The example sites should be clearly labelled as examples so they don't read as "trusted by" customer logos.

## Prioritized recommendations for the GlobalTest page

The table orders the changes by expected impact relative to effort. "Evidence type" separates controlled studies (**Data**) from observed competitor practice (**Pattern**) and our own judgement (**Opinion**). Impact estimates are qualitative. None of the cited effect sizes was measured on a free geo-testing tool, so GlobalTest should measure each change with its own analytics: test completion rate, share-link clicks and return visits.

| # | Recommendation | Evidence behind it | Evidence type | Expected impact | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Replace the elapsed-time bar with a **live per-country stage list** driven by backend events: Queued → Starting Chrome in {country} → Loading {domain} → Capturing screenshot → Recording video → Done. Add one line explaining why: "We start a real Chrome browser in {country}; the first start takes ~30 s." | Transparent waiting held 62–63% preference vs 42%/23% for a plain bar at 30/60 s; explained waits feel shorter (Buell & Norton; Maister) | Data | High: likely the largest single lever on test completion | Medium (needs status events from the backend) |
| 2 | Reshape the progress: jump to a visible amount as soon as the test starts, move between real checkpoints, never creep at 95–99%, and change the copy to "usually 30–60 s" | Fast-early bars reduce drop-off and slow starts increase it; end stalls hurt most; estimates should be generous (Villar; Harrison; NN/g) | Data | Medium | Small |
| 3 | Show each country the moment it finishes, and draw attention to the first one that lands | Streaming partial results in CI and AI-image tools; BrowserStack fills in thumbnails as they finish | Pattern | Medium–high: gives value at ~30 s, when transparency's benefit starts to fade | Small–medium |
| 4 | Put status in the tab title and favicon ("(2/3) GlobalTest"), add a subtle completion cue, offer an opt-in "Notify me when done" button, and announce progress through `aria-live` | Users switch tasks after 10 s (NN/g); notification permission needs a user gesture (Braze) | Data for the problem; Pattern/Opinion for the fix | Medium: recovers visitors who switch tabs | Small |
| 5 | Design failure states: say plainly what happened (timeout, block, error page), when it happened and why, and offer one-click retry for that country | Transparency backfires when the outcome is bad (Buell & Norton Exp. 5) | Data | Medium: protects trust | Small–medium |
| 6 | Add a one-line results summary above the cards: countries loaded, fastest and slowest, redirects, language or status differences. Show localization as facts, not penalties | Performance tools lead with one judgement; no geo competitor summarizes differences | Pattern + Opinion | Medium: makes results quick to scan and gives the share preview its text | Medium |
| 7 | Add a **Re-test** button on results and "Recent tests" stored in localStorage (no account) | Observatory's scan, fix and rescan loop took 2,500+ sites from F to A/A+ | Pattern (observational) | Medium for return visits | Small |
| 8 | Show a real example result above the fold (a small strip of three countries) and a "Try an example" link under the field. Load examples instantly from cache, have "Run this test" pre-fill the form, and label them "Example: cnn.com" | ~57% of viewing time is above the fold; leaving is most likely in the first 10 s (NN/g); demo vendor claims (Navattic) | Data for attention; vendor data for demos | Medium: converts visitors who aren't ready to test their own site | Small–medium |
| 9 | Make URL entry forgiving: accept bare domains and add `https://` automatically; use `inputmode="url"` and `enterkeyhint="go"`; turn off autocapitalize and autocorrect; use 16px+ text and a visible label; validate on blur; stack a full-width button under ~480px | Baymard, NN/g placeholders, mobile input guidance, Unbounce mobile traffic | Data + Guideline | Medium on mobile, small on desktop | Small |
| 10 | **Shareable result links** (unlisted by default) with a server-rendered OG image of the country screenshots plus the summary line. Keep the summary and thumbnails longer than the 1-day video retention. Expired links should open a page that pre-fills a re-run | Wordle and HubSpot growth from sharing; GTmetrix permanent reports; OG image click data (older) | Pattern + self-reported case studies | High for acquisition and SEO, but unproven for GlobalTest | Large (storage, retention policy, privacy) |
| 11 | After results, offer an optional email: "Re-check weekly and alert me if a country breaks, slows down or changes" | GTmetrix and UptimeRobot monitoring loops; NN/g against login walls; GTmetrix gating backlash | Pattern + Data (login walls) | High for return visits if adopted; adoption rate unknown | Large (scheduler, email, diffing) |
| 12 | Collapse the country chips and output cards into one summary line under the button, for example "US · UK · Germany · Screenshot · Change", and show video size hints on mobile | Progressive disclosure (NN/g); GTmetrix collapsed defaults; choice-overload meta-analysis shows the count of options matters less than how much they dominate the view | Guideline + Pattern | Small–medium | Small |
| 13 | Add trust microcopy beside the field: "Free, no sign-up. Public pages only. Results deleted after 1 day." | Our own reasoning; no study on privacy notes near URL inputs | Opinion | Small–medium | Small |
| 14 | Turn the map into live status: pins pulse while running and show thumbnails when done. Make pins keyboard-reachable or mark the map as decorative. Shrink or hide it on phones | Operational transparency applied to a visual; no direct study | Opinion | Small–medium | Medium |
| 15 | Hold off on usage counters, a single grade that penalizes localization, and watermarked screenshots until they can be A/B tested | Mixed social-proof results (GoodUI); 70% of hero tests show no difference (Mida) | Data (mixed) + Opinion | Avoids possible harm | none |

The first five items form a single "waiting experience" project and should ship together. Items 7–9 are small and independent. Items 10 and 11 require a product decision about the 1-day retention policy and should be planned as one: share links bring new visitors, and monitoring brings existing ones back.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the problem from "how do we entertain people for a minute" to "how do we make the minute itself proof of value". GlobalTest's wait comes from real browsers starting in real countries, and that is the product's main differentiator over geo-IP guesses. Showing that work live is both the strongest-evidenced engagement fix and the clearest statement of what makes the tool different. The bigger strategic gap is after the wait: every growth loop observed among comparable tools depends on results that last longer than a day, either as a link someone shares or as a re-check that sends an email next week.

Two uncertainties remain. No source measures how often visitors of free tools return, and the free-tool growth figures are self-reported, so the retention recommendations are reasoned bets rather than proven gains. And the 1-day deletion policy is presumably a cost and privacy choice. If it stays, GlobalTest should invest in making each single visit complete, with a summary, a re-test button and recent tests in the browser. If it relaxes even for lightweight summaries, shareable links and monitoring become the main ways GlobalTest can grow.
